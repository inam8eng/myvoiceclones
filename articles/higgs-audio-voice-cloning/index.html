Higgs Audio voice cloning: free for monetized videos if you credit it, but not for apps

TechBy Inam·November 9, 2026
The short answer

Higgs Audio v3, also called Higgs TTS 3, is a free download from Boson AI that clones a voice from a short clip and speaks over 100 languages. Its licence is research and non commercial, with one exception. Creators can publish and monetize videos, podcasts and audiobooks on their own channels for free, as long as they credit Boson AI's Higgs Audio. Apps, APIs and resale need a paid licence.

You want a clean voice for your YouTube videos, so you look up the rules for Higgs Audio. One website tells you it uses the Apache 2.0 licence and is free for any kind of work, and another tells you it is only for research and you must pay for a commercial agreement. I see why people get confused here, because both pages are talking about different pieces of the same software.

You can monetize videos made with Higgs Audio v3 on your own channel for free, but you have to credit Boson AI's Higgs Audio where people can see it. That rule only applies to your own published content. If you want to put the model inside an app, a website or a paid service, you need a separate licence from Boson.

What is Higgs Audio?

Higgs Audio is a set of speech models built by Boson AI to turn written sentences into spoken audio, and they can copy a person's voice from a recording. Version 2 came out in July 2025 and ran on about 3 billion parameters, which are the internal numbers a model adjusts while it learns how speech sounds. Version 3 arrived on Hugging Face in June 2026, and that one increased the size to about 4 billion.

The company gave it two names, but Higgs Audio v3 and Higgs TTS 3 point to the exact same model. The GitHub repository calls it Higgs Audio v3, while the Higgs TTS 3 model card on Hugging Face uses Higgs TTS 3. The file you download takes up 9.3 GB on your drive, and it generates audio at 24 kHz.

When version 3 arrived, the setup changed as well. The Higgs Audio GitHub page now tells you not to clone that repository for this newest release, because version 3 runs without the code stored there.

Why do some pages say Apache 2.0 and others say non commercial?

The confusion happens because the code and the model weights are two separate things with their own legal terms. The code on GitHub uses the Apache 2.0 licence, which lets you build commercial tools, and GitHub displays that badge right at the top. But the model weights are the actual brain that turns text into speech, and those files follow a separate set of rules on Hugging Face.

So if someone only checks the GitHub badge, they write that the tool is Apache 2.0, and if they only read the top of the model page, they write that it is strictly for research. Here is what each part really uses.

Go by the licence on the model file, not the badge on the GitHub page.

Can you monetize YouTube videos made with Higgs Audio?

Yes, you can, and this is the specific detail that many online summaries miss. If you open Section II-A of the Higgs TTS 3 licence, you will find a section called the Creator Use Grant. It gives you permission as a creator to publish and earn money from podcasts, videos, audiobooks and social posts on channels you own or control, and it names ad-supported, sponsored and subscription channels.

Boson adds one clear condition here called the Creator Acknowledgment. You must give credit to Boson AI's Higgs Audio, either by speaking the line aloud in your audio track or by writing it in the text that goes with the content, such as your YouTube description box or your podcast notes. The licence makes it plain that this note must be easy for people to see, and you cannot hide it down at the bottom of the credits.

The wording Boson suggests is: "This audio was created with Boson AI's Higgs Audio", followed by their link to boson.ai/higgs-audio. Paste that line near the top of your video description on every video. That copy and paste takes ten seconds, and if you leave it out, you are outside the permission.

What does the Creator Use Grant not cover?

This grant gives you permission to produce and publish your own media. It does not allow you to host the model on a server as an API, resell it, or build it into a text to speech, dubbing or content creation product that other people use. If you plan to build any of those, the agreement says you must go to Boson for a separate commercial licence.

Take a close look at the words "channels you own or control", because that condition matters more than it looks. If you work as a freelancer and make a voiceover that gets published on a client's channel, that channel is not yours. My reading is that client work falls outside this creator grant, so ask Boson before you take client work with Higgs Audio v3.

The licence also sets out rules that every creator must follow. You are not allowed to clone a real person's voice without their explicit consent, you cannot use the generated speech to train another speech model, and you must add an AI label wherever a platform rule or the law asks for one.

Is Higgs Audio v2 different?

Yes, and its terms are actually looser in one way. Version 2 runs under the Boson Higgs Audio 2 Community License, which is based on the Meta Llama 3 community licence. It has no Creator Acknowledgment rule for paid work, but if your products have more than 100,000 annual active users, you have to ask Boson for an expanded licence.

If you run an ordinary YouTube channel, you will not come close to that limit. The real problem is the hardware it asks for. The official version 2 instructions advise a graphics card with at least 24 GB of memory for best performance, and the developers have already moved on to version 3.

I should also mention a detail for people using OmniVoice. If you check issue 184, a team running a monetized YouTube channel asked Boson how this 100,000 user rule applies to them, because OmniVoice uses the Higgs audio tokenizer. When I checked that ticket, it was still sitting open without an answer from Boson.

How do you clone a voice with Higgs Audio v3?

To clone a voice, you give the model a clear recording of the speaker and, ideally, a written transcript of what they said. Boson notes on the model card that giving it the exact text materially improves how close the clone sounds. People skip this step because it takes extra effort, but it is free quality.

You have three practical ways to set this up, and each one handles your audio differently.

The model card does not list an exact length for the reference clip, so I will not guess one. The Windows app built by the community automatically cuts every reference down to its first 30 seconds, which shows that it expects a short and clean recording. From my own cloning, I suggest one or two natural sentences in a quiet room with no background music and no room echo.

Can you run Higgs Audio on Windows?

Yes, you can run it through a community project called Higgs Audio v3 Studio, which was not made by Boson. It runs a C++ build of the model directly on your NVIDIA graphics card using CUDA, so you do not have to touch Python, and it lets you record your audio sample inside the app and can write the transcript for you using Whisper.

The software targets Windows 11, also lists Windows 10, and asks for an NVIDIA graphics card that supports CUDA 13. The app's page says running the model purely on an ordinary processor is not the focus of this app, so if you have no NVIDIA card, this is not your route.

There is a legal point here that I want to be straight about. The app downloads converted copies of the model, and the licence treats a quantized model, which is a file stored to use less memory, as a Derivative Work. The Creator Use Grant was written for Boson's own materials, and the app's README also tells you to check the model licence before commercial use. If money depends on it, use the official model files or ask Boson.

How much video memory does Higgs Audio v3 need?

Boson does not publish a minimum for a home PC. The most useful numbers come from the community Windows app, which offers the model at five sizes. Quantization is a method where you store the model with less precision so the whole thing fits into less memory, and the lower settings use less.

If you have a 12 GB card, start with Q8_0. The app's README notes that memory use can jump for a moment while it generates speech, so a card that barely meets the number can run into trouble. On the older version 2 release, a person noted in issue 117 that it was complex and slow on an 8 GB card.

Is Higgs Audio v3 fast?

On a data centre card the model runs very fast, but that is the only speed number Boson has published. Their model card reports a real time factor of 0.147 with one request at a time on one NVIDIA H100. Real time factor measures how long it takes to make one second of audio, so 0.147 means well under a fifth of a second of work for each second of speech.

An H100 is a server card meant for data centres, not a card you have in a home computer, so do not expect those speeds on your desk. I could not find a published figure for an everyday gaming card, so run a short test sentence on your own system first.

Does Higgs Audio v3 work well in English?

English belongs to the most accurate group of languages in this model. The model card says it reaches single digit word or character error rates across 102 languages, with 85 languages under 5 percent and 17 sitting between 5 and 10 percent. An error rate here counts how many words out of 100 came out wrong when the audio was checked.

Keep in mind that these numbers come from Boson's own tests, so treat them as a starting point. The bigger reason to pick this model over smaller tools is that it supports inline tags, which let you write emotion, style, pauses and sound effects directly into your script.

When should you pick something else?

Higgs Audio v3 is a solid option if you publish on your own channels, do not mind pasting their credit line, and want many languages or emotion tags. It does not work well if you take client projects or want to build a product, because then you are paying Boson or asking them first.

If you do not want to add a credit to your videos, VoxCPM2 and Qwen3-TTS are free models that allow paid work without one. If you ended up here because Coqui XTTS cannot be used for paid work, this is a similar question, but Boson has a friendlier answer for creators. If you only need English on a Windows machine, I still suggest setting up GPT-SoVITS on Windows first, because its MIT licence has no credit condition and no limit on client work.

FAQ

Is Higgs Audio free for commercial use?

It is free only for certain uses, because Higgs Audio v3 uses a research and non commercial licence. But its Creator Use Grant allows creators to earn money from videos, podcasts and audiobooks on their own channels if they credit Boson AI's Higgs Audio. If you want to put the model inside an app, run an API or resell it, you need a paid licence from Boson.

Do I have to credit Higgs Audio on YouTube?

Yes, if you use Higgs Audio v3 under the terms of the Creator Use Grant. You can speak the credit inside the audio or paste it in the video description box, and it has to sit where viewers can easily see it rather than buried at the bottom of the credits.

Can Higgs Audio v3 run on Windows?

Yes, you can run it through a community tool called Higgs Audio v3 Studio, which requires an NVIDIA card with CUDA 13 support. The app was not made by Boson, and its recommended version asks for 12 GB of video memory.

Is Higgs Audio the same as Higgs TTS 3?

Yes, Boson uses both of these names for the third version of their voice model, and the Hugging Face page is called higgs-tts-3-4b. Version 2 is an older release with a different licence.
