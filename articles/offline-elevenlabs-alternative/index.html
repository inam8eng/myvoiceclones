An offline ElevenLabs alternative that runs on your own PC

MoneyBy Inam·August 26, 2026
The short answer

If you want an ElevenLabs alternative that is offline and subscription free, a local tool like VoiceClone runs the cloning on your own Windows PC: $49 once instead of monthly plans, nothing uploaded, English only. ElevenLabs remains stronger for multiple languages and phone based workflows.

I used ElevenLabs before I built anything of my own, and I want to start by being fair to it, because most "alternative" articles are written by people trying to knife the market leader. The voices are genuinely good. The website is smooth. If a friend needed Spanish narration by tonight from a phone, I would point them there without hesitating.

And still I left, for two reasons that had nothing to do with quality: my voice lived on their servers, and my card got charged every month for access to it. If either of those sentences bothers you too, this is the honest version of the comparison I wish I had read earlier.

The two ways to run a voice clone

Every voice tool sits on one side of a line. Either the model runs on the company's servers, which means your voice recording goes up and your audio comes down, or the model runs on your own computer, which means nothing goes anywhere.

ElevenLabs is the first kind, and it is excellent at being that kind. VoiceClone, the tool I built and the reason this site exists, is the second kind: the engine, an open source model called GPT-SoVITS, runs on your Windows PC, the free version lets you clone your own voice and hear it, and a $49 one time license opens everything else. No account, no upload, no monthly anything.

Where ElevenLabs genuinely wins

I will give you the real list, not a token one:

  • Languages. ElevenLabs speaks dozens of them well. VoiceClone speaks English, full stop. If you need anything else, stop reading and use the cloud tool.
  • Zero hardware requirements. Their servers do the work, so a phone or an old laptop is enough. Local cloning wants a real computer, and an NVIDIA graphics card to be quick about it.
  • Instant everything. No setup download, no model files. You sign up and generate within minutes.
  • A voice library. Thousands of stock voices ready to use. VoiceClone ships 4 trained preset voices and expects the main voice to be yours.

Where a local tool wins

  • Privacy that is structural, not promised. ElevenLabs has policies about your data. A local tool does not need policies, because there is no server to have policies about. Your recording stays a file on your disk. For me this stopped being abstract the day I realized a voice is a biometric, like a fingerprint you cannot change.
  • Cost shape. Their Creator plan runs about $22 a month as I write this, and heavy use costs more, which is a few hundred dollars a year forever. $49 once is a different kind of decision, and after month three it is simply less money.
  • No meter anxiety. Credits make you accept the almost right take. On your own machine, five retries cost nothing, so VoiceClone generates several takes for every line by default, listens to each with a speech recognizer, and gives you the best one word by word. A metered service cannot hand out compute that freely.
  • Word level repair. When one word comes out wrong in a long clip, I click that word, regenerate just it, or record the pronunciation myself once, and the fix splices in with the rest untouched. On cloud tools the usual answer is regenerate the block and spend more credits.
  • Real training, included. ElevenLabs' professional cloning runs on their infrastructure and their terms. VoiceClone's Make it perfect button trains a true copy of your voice on your own graphics card, under an hour on a normal card, and the trained model is a file you own. I measured my own voice going from an 80ish percent speaker match instant clone to about 96 percent after training, and that trained voice is what narrates my actual videos.
  • It keeps working if I disappear. Harsh but true test for any tool: if the company vanishes tomorrow, a cloud voice is gone, a local app keeps running on your machine, offline, forever.

The quality question, answered honestly

For English, a trained local clone of your own voice will sound more like you than a cloud instant clone, because training beats instant cloning every time, and locally the training is included instead of gatekept. For instant clones of arbitrary voices, ElevenLabs' big models are still the smoothest I have heard. So the practical answer depends on what you are doing: narrating your own content in your own voice, local wins on likeness and price. Producing many different stock voices across languages, cloud wins on range.

The one limit I repeat everywhere so nobody buys surprised: VoiceClone is English only, it cannot sing, and on a PC without a graphics card it works but slowly. There is a free tier exactly so you can hear your own clone before paying anything.

FAQ

Is there a free ElevenLabs alternative?

Several open source engines are free if you can set up Python yourself, GPT-SoVITS among them. VoiceClone packages that class of engine into a normal Windows app: free to clone one voice and hear it, $49 once for unlimited use. No subscription either way.

Can ElevenLabs run offline?

No. ElevenLabs is a cloud service, every generation happens on their servers. For offline generation you need a locally running tool such as VoiceClone or a self hosted open source engine.

Is local voice cloning as good as ElevenLabs?

For English and your own voice, a locally trained clone typically matches you more closely than a cloud instant clone. For other languages and stock voices, ElevenLabs is ahead. Recording quality matters more than the brand in both cases.

What does ElevenLabs cost per year versus a one time tool?

Their commonly used Creator tier is about $22 monthly, roughly $264 a year, ongoing. A one time tool like VoiceClone is $49 total. Check current prices on their site, plans change.

Try both, decide with your ears

Do the test I did: take the same two minute recording of yourself, make a clone in their trial and in the free VoiceClone app, and generate the same paragraph. Listen with your eyes closed. One of the two results will make the decision for you, and either way you decided from evidence, which beats any comparison article, including mine.
