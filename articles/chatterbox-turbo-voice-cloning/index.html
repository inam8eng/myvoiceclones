Chatterbox Turbo: the free voice cloner that can laugh and sigh, what it drops from the original, and how to run it

TechBy Inam·November 25, 2026
The short answer

Chatterbox Turbo is a free English text to speech model from Resemble AI, and it has 350 million parameters. You give it one voice clip longer than 5 seconds to copy a voice, and you can put tags like [laugh] and [sigh] inside your script. Unlike the original Chatterbox, it ignores the exaggeration and cfg_weight settings. Every audio file it makes has the Perth watermark in it, the licence is MIT, and Resemble AI retired the project in August 2026.

You might already be using Chatterbox, or maybe you heard a short clip of an AI voice laughing mid sentence and started seeing the name Chatterbox Turbo everywhere. Then you open the GitHub page and see five models listed side by side, with no plain answer on which one you should pick or what you give up by choosing the faster one.

Pick Chatterbox Turbo if you want fast English speech in a copied voice with a laugh or a sigh in it. Stay with the original Chatterbox if you want the emotion setting, because Turbo ignores it. Here I will walk through what Turbo actually is, which tags it understands, what kind of voice clip it needs, and a few small traps in the files that come with the project.

What is Chatterbox Turbo?

Chatterbox Turbo belongs to the Chatterbox family on GitHub, a set of text to speech models built by Resemble AI. Text to speech just means you type a line and the computer reads it out loud, and in this case Turbo reads it in a voice copied from a short recording. Resemble put up its model page on Hugging Face on 2 December 2025, and it is free under the MIT licence, so you can use it for paid work.

Between the two main English models, Turbo is the lighter one. It sits at 350 million parameters, which is a rough way of measuring how heavy an AI model is, while the original Chatterbox has 500 million. Resemble also says it brought the final stage, the part that turns the planned speech into actual sound, down from 10 steps to one, and that was the slow part in the older model. Just like the original, Turbo only speaks English.

Before you build any real work around it, there is one important catch. Right at the top of the GitHub page, there is a notice saying that as of August 2026 Chatterbox is retired research and is no longer maintained. When I checked on 25 November 2026, the repository had about 28,100 stars and 385 open issues, so a lot of people still use it, but nobody at Resemble is promising any fixes. If you want the background on why the company shifted direction, I covered it in what happened to Resemble AI.

Which tags can Chatterbox Turbo read, like laugh and sigh?

Most people look at Turbo because of its tags. A tag is simply a word inside square brackets that you drop into your script, and instead of reading that word out loud, the model produces that sound in the cloned voice right at that spot. Resemble calls these paralinguistic tags, which is just a technical name for the sounds a person makes that are not actual words.

In the README, the project names three of them and adds "and more". If you look at the project's own demo file, it puts nine of them on buttons: [clear throat], [sigh], [shush], [cough], [groan], [sniff], [gasp], [chuckle] and [laugh]. You place the tag right where you want the sound, so a line in your script looks like this: Oh, that is a good one [chuckle] anyway, here is the price list.

I would stick to those nine as the safe list. Because the project does not show any other tags anywhere, I would not rely on making up your own.

What does Turbo drop from the original Chatterbox?

Here is something the model pages do not spell out clearly. People who used the original Chatterbox usually know it for two main settings: exaggeration, which controls how dramatic the voice sounds, and cfg_weight, which roughly controls the pacing and how closely the speech sticks to your reference clip. I went through both of those in my Chatterbox review.

Turbo simply ignores them. When I read the Turbo code on GitHub, I saw that if you pass exaggeration, cfg_weight, or a third setting called min_p, the code prints a warning saying those settings are not supported by the Turbo version and will be ignored, and then it keeps going. That means if you follow an older Chatterbox tutorial and push exaggeration up to 0.7, nothing at all happens on Turbo.

On Turbo, emotion comes from the tags and from the tone of your recording, and the exaggeration setting does nothing. If your reference clip sounds flat, Turbo will read your script in a flat voice too, so record your sample in the same mood you want in the final audio.

How long should the voice clip be for Chatterbox Turbo?

Your voice clip has to be longer than 5 seconds, and making it much longer than that does not really help. The first thing the code does is check your clip length, and if it is 5 seconds or less, it stops and tells you that the audio prompt must be longer than 5 seconds. That single error message is what trips up a lot of people on their first try.

On the upper end, the code trims your clip before using it. The part that generates the sound only looks at the first 10 seconds, and the part that plans the speech only looks at the first 15 seconds, while the whole file is just used to pull a general fingerprint of the voice. Because of that, a 3 minute recording gives Turbo very little extra over a clean 15 second clip, and the first 10 seconds are what matter most.

Give Turbo a clean clip of 10 to 15 seconds, and make sure the best part is at the start. Leave out any background music, start speaking right away, and make sure only one person is talking. Everything else I wrote about recording a sample that clones well applies here as well.

There is another small trap sitting inside the example file. The project includes a script called example_tts_turbo.py, and by default the line that loads your voice clip is turned off. If you run it as it is, it just uses a built in voice from the model download, so do not judge the cloning quality from that first run. Put your own file name into the audio_prompt_path line, just like the README shows.

How do you run Chatterbox Turbo on your PC?

Installing Turbo is no different from the rest of the family, because it lives inside the same Python package. You run pip install chatterbox-tts, though on Windows the order of steps matters if you have an NVIDIA card, so follow my guide on installing Chatterbox on Windows first and then come back here. The first time you load Turbo, it pulls the model files down from Hugging Face, and when I added them up they came to about 4 GB.

Once it is installed, you have two ways to run it. You can either use a short Python script that loads the model, takes your text and voice clip, and saves a wav file at 24,000 Hz, or you can use the project's small browser interface from GitHub called gradio_tts_turbo_app.py. That browser page gives you a text box marked for 300 characters at most, the nine tag buttons, and an option to upload or record your clip.

Before you launch that browser page, open the file and check the very last line. It starts up with the share option turned on, which tells Gradio, the tool that builds the page, to create a public web link to your PC. Change share=True to share=False so the page stays local on your own computer.

To be plain with you, I did not run Turbo on my own PC tonight. Everything I wrote here comes from reading the project's pages and going through its code, so test it with your own voice clip before you plan a video around it.

What about Chatterbox Nano, the smaller one?

Nano uses the same design as Turbo in a smaller 110 million parameter size, and it is built for a PC without a graphics card. Resemble says it runs about 3 times faster than real time on 8 processor cores. It supports the same tags, it also ignores the exaggeration setting, and the download is about 3 GB.

The catch is how you actually install it. The package pip gives you is version 0.1.7 from 26 March 2026, while Nano's model page only went up on 14 April 2026, and when I opened that package I found that its Turbo file has no Nano option inside it. Because of that, the README line for loading Nano only works if you install directly from the GitHub source using git clone and then running pip install -e . inside the folder.

If you installed Chatterbox through pip, you have Turbo and you do not have Nano yet.

Is Chatterbox Turbo good enough for YouTube voiceovers?

If you need short English lines with a bit of life in them, Turbo is a fair option and it costs nothing. Resemble points to listening tests on a site called Podonos that compare Turbo against ElevenLabs Turbo v2.5, Cartesia Sonic 3 and VibeVoice 7B. Since those are tests the maker chose to highlight, treat them as a claim and listen to the audio samples yourself.

If you are making content, there are two limits you should keep in mind. First, every audio file Turbo creates carries Resemble's Perth watermark, which is a hidden signal you cannot hear that a small script can detect later, meaning anyone with that script can tell the audio came from Chatterbox. Second, because Turbo copies a voice from just a few seconds without any training, it catches the general sound of the voice but loses a lot of the person's real accent and talking rhythm.

That second limit is the reason I built VoiceClone on a different engine called GPT-SoVITS, which trains on 1 to 3 minutes of your own talking. VoiceClone is English only and does not have laugh tags, so if your script is full of reactions, Turbo can do something my app cannot. When you need a long narration that genuinely sounds like you, though, training wins. VoiceClone launches from a single file called START VoiceClone.bat, runs offline on Windows 10 and 11, lets you clone and fully train one voice for free with 3 test generations, and costs $49 once for the full licence.

Make sure you only clone your own voice, or the voice of someone who has clearly given you permission.

Use Turbo for short, lively English lines with a laugh in them. Use a trained voice when you need long narration in your own voice.

FAQ

Is Chatterbox Turbo free for commercial use?

Yes, both the code and the model use the MIT licence, so you can use them for paid work. Just remember that Resemble AI retired the project in August 2026, so there is no support, and every file it generates carries the Perth watermark.

Does Chatterbox Turbo support other languages?

No, both Turbo and Nano speak English only. If you need other languages, the same family includes Chatterbox Multilingual V3, which the README lists as supporting 23 languages.

Can Chatterbox Turbo run without a graphics card?

The code lets you choose the processor as your device, so it will start. Even so, Resemble points anyone without a graphics card toward Nano, the 110 million parameter model, which it says runs about 3 times faster than real time on 8 processor cores.

Why does Chatterbox Turbo say the audio prompt must be longer than 5 seconds?

That message pops up because your reference clip is 5 seconds or shorter, and the Turbo code will not accept a clip that short. Record a clean clip that is 10 to 15 seconds long and try again.
